Abstract

Agricultural price volatility represents one of the most critical socio-economic vulnerabilities across developing economies, particularly in India where agriculture contributes significantly to national GDP and sustains over 58% of rural households. Farmers persistently suffer from information asymmetry, localized market gluts, post-harvest distress selling, and absence of predictive market intelligence, resulting in substantial financial losses while intermediaries capture disproportionate profits.

This project presents AgriPrice AI, a full-stack, enterprise-grade agricultural intelligence platform that unifies multi-step recursive deep learning time-series price forecasting, agro-climatic sensor telemetry, interstate APMC mandi arbitrage optimization, and cold storage financial modeling into a unified software system.

The computational core employs a hybrid ensemble architecture comprising a Bi-directional Long Short-Term Memory (Bi-LSTM) recurrent neural network with Multi-Head Self-Attention, combined with a Random Forest Regressor and trigonometric seasonal harmonics (sin/cos day-of-year transformations). The engine is trained on over 6,840 daily verified APMC benchmark transactions spanning 16 key agricultural commodities across 12 major Indian states (Maharashtra, Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, Karnataka, Rajasthan, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal, Haryana, and Telangana).

Empirical validation yields an R² determination coefficient of 94.8% with an RMSE of 78.49 INR/quintal and MAE of 60.21 INR/quintal. The platform features 9 dedicated analytical modules accessible via a persistent enterprise navigation bar, interactive IoT maturity & soil moisture simulations, Dijkstra-based interstate transport arbitrage routing, cold storage break-even analytics, and an automated SQLite farmer price threshold alert engine deployed live on Vercel.

Keywords: Agricultural Price Forecasting, Bi-LSTM, Deep Learning, Multi-Head Self-Attention, APMC Mandi, IoT Smart Harvesting, Interstate Arbitrage, Cold Storage Economics, FastAPI, React 18.

Chapter 1: Introduction

1.1 Background & Context of Agricultural Markets in India

Agriculture forms the structural backbone of the Indian economy. While technological advancements have enhanced on-field crop yields, post-harvest agricultural marketing has remained constrained by institutional fragmentation and severe price volatility. The marketing of agricultural produce operates largely through the Agricultural Produce Market Committee (APMC) mandi network, where auction bidding by commission agents often suffers from cartelization and transparency deficits.

Perishable crops such as tomatoes, onions, and potatoes routinely experience price swings of 200% to 400% within a single crop cycle. Farmers face market crashes during bumper harvest influxes and sky-high retail prices during supply crunches, suffering massive financial distress due to the lack of predictive intelligence.

1.2 Problem Statement & Industry Motivation

The traditional paradigm of agricultural decision-making relies on retrospective intuition and word-of-mouth. Key industry challenges addressed include:

  • Information Asymmetry: Wholesale aggregators exploit smallholder farmers who lack visibility into price movements.
  • Inadequacy of Traditional Econometric Models: Static linear models (ARIMA) fail to capture sudden climate shocks and arrival gluts.
  • Absence of Integrated Agro-Sensor Feedback: Market price predictors operate disconnected from on-field soil moisture and crop maturity sensors.
  • Sub-optimal Interstate Arbitrage: Farmers cannot capitalize on huge inter-state price differentials due to lack of freight cost optimization.

1.3 Project Objectives

  • Design and train a hybrid deep learning model combining Bi-LSTM, Multi-Head Self-Attention, and Random Forest achieving R² > 92%.
  • Build a comprehensive relational database of 6,840+ verified APMC auction records across 16 crops and 12 states.
  • Create an IoT sensor harvesting advisor mapping soil moisture and Brix maturity to optimal harvest dates.

  • Develop a cold storage break-even financial analyzer and SQLite price threshold alert system.
  • Deploy the full-stack web application on Vercel serverless cloud infrastructure.

Chapter 2: Literature Survey

2.1 Traditional Econometric Forecasting vs Modern Deep Learning

Traditional econometric time-series models (such as ARIMA, SARIMA, and Holt-Winters) operate on strict assumptions of linearity and stationary error distributions. When subjected to agricultural commodities with sudden climate anomalies or logistical disruptions, these models fail to maintain predictive validity beyond a few days.

Deep Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Bidirectional LSTMs overcome these barriers by preserving past and future sequence context through gated cell states. Multi-Head Self-Attention mechanisms further eliminate recurrence bottlenecks, enabling direct attribution of long-range harvest shocks.

Chapter 8 & 9: Deployment & Conclusion

8.1 Cloud Deployment on Vercel

The platform is deployed live at https://market-price-one.vercel.app. Using serverless routing rules in vercel.json, static assets are served instantly from Vercel's global CDN while API requests execute via ephemeral AWS Lambda serverless functions. To overcome read-only serverless filesystems, the database dynamically initializes in /tmp/agri_market.db.
